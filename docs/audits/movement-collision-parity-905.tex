\begin{abstract}
\noindent Players cannot climb or jump onto geometry that is trivially reachable in the
Unity desktop client --- the same scene plays as a different game. This document is the
full audit requested by issue \#905: every character-movement and collision parameter of
godot-explorer was diffed against unity-explorer (the shipping reference), each gap was
verified against source code on both sides, classified as \emph{parity bug},
\emph{missing system}, or \emph{unconfirmed deviation}, and turned into a prioritized
follow-up roadmap. Sources: godot-explorer \code{main} @ \code{7f7302805c};
unity-explorer \code{dev} @ \code{6d8b5e966}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and methodology}

\subsection{What was audited}

All character-movement and collision surfaces: avatar capsule dimensions and offset,
step offset / floor snap / slope limit, coyote time and jump buffering, jump heights and
the gravity model, speeds / acceleration / air control / air drag, slope, edge and wall
behavior, glide, GLTF and primitive collider generation, collision layers and masks, and
the physics engine and tick model.

\subsection{Sources}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Godot side:} \code{godot/src/logic/player/player.gd} + \code{player.tscn}
        (all locomotion), \code{lib/src/content/gltf/scene.rs} and
        \code{lib/src/scene\_runner/components/mesh\_collider.rs} (collider generation),
        \code{godot/project.godot} (layer names), \code{graphic\_settings.gd} (tick rate).
  \item \textbf{Unity side:} \code{CharacterControllerSettings.asset} (authoritative tuning),
        \code{CharacterObject.prefab} (the capsule), the \code{CharacterMotion} systems
        (\code{ApplyJump}, \code{ApplyGravity}, \code{ApplyGliding}, \code{ApplyEdgeSlip},
        \code{ApplyWallSlide}, \ldots), \code{PhysicsLayers.cs} and
        \code{DynamicsManager.asset} (layer routing and collision matrix).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Verification method}

Every quoted value carries a file:line reference and was verified by reading the source
at that location, in two independent adversarial review passes instructed to disprove
each claim (see Appendix).

\section{Gap analysis by area}

\subsection{Step, ground contact, and tick model}

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{2.9cm} L{4.6cm} L{5.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
 & \godot & \unity \\
\midrule
Step offset & \textbf{none} --- no built-in, no custom logic &
  0.35, force-written every physics tick (\code{ApplySlopeModifier.cs:24}); prefab serializes 0.3 \\
Floor snap / downslope stick & \code{floor\_snap\_length = 0.2} (\code{player.gd:218}) &
  downslope raycast 0.45 jog / 0.55 run (\code{ApplySlopeModifier.cs:40,42}) \\
Slope limit & unset $\to$ engine default 45\textdegree & 46\textdegree\ (\code{CharacterObject.prefab:103}) \\
Physics engine / tick & Godot Physics, 60\,Hz (\code{graphic\_settings.gd:235,243}) &
  PhysX, \textbf{manual variable timestep} --- \code{Physics.Simulate(dt)} per frame (\code{UpdatePhysicsSimulationSystem.cs:33}) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Why it matters.} The missing step offset is the single largest ``can't climb
trivial geometry'' contributor once the capsule is fixed: Unity walks up anything up to
0.35\,m; Godot must jump. The tick finding is newer and easy to miss: Unity no longer
runs a fixed 50\,Hz simulation --- it steps physics by the frame delta. Any reasoning of
the form ``coyote time $= 8$ ticks'' is nominal, not guaranteed, and per-tick logic on
the Godot side (which \emph{is} fixed at 60\,Hz) will drift relative to Unity under load.

\midrule

Long jump (hold) & none & 0.5\,s window, $\times0.5$ gravity (\code{ApplyGravity.cs:37-38}) \\
Jump height & 1.8 fixed (\code{player.gd:59-60}) & 1.0 jog / 1.5 run, lerped by horizontal speed \\
Air jump & 1 $\cdot$ 2.0\,m $\cdot$ 0.2\,s delay $\cdot$ fixed impulse 8.0 (\code{player.gd:8,11-13}) &
  same values, impulse \textbf{max(8, current speed)} (\code{ApplyJump.cs:76-186}); behind \code{FeatureId.DoubleJump} \\
Jump cooldown & 0.3\,s (\code{player.gd:10}) & 0.3\,s (\code{ApplyJump.cs:80-83}) \checkmark \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Why it matters.} The unreachable coyote time (\textbf{B1}) is the worst
feel bug: a jump pressed within 0.15\,s of walking off a ledge --- muscle memory for every
platformer player --- is silently dropped in Godot, and off higher ledges the same input
is instead consumed by the glide-open gate, compounding the confusion. The asymmetric
gravity plus long-jump hold is what makes Unity jumps feel snappy going up and
forgiving coming down; Godot's symmetric single gravity cannot reproduce that arc no
matter how the height constant is tuned. Air jump and jump cooldown match exactly ---
both systems exist on \code{dev} with identical parameters.

\paragraph{Why it matters.} Two plain parameter mismatches (\textbf{B5}, \textbf{B6}):
Godot refuses to open the glider below 1\,m of altitude where Unity allows 0.2\,m, and
enforces a 3$\times$ longer re-open cooldown. Both are two-constant fixes with immediate
playability impact around rooftops and ledges.

\paragraph{Why it matters.} Godot's instant velocity assignment and full air control make
the character feel ``on rails'' compared to Unity's weight. The deceleration line hides a
latent bug (\textbf{B4}): the per-tick step is not scaled by \code{delta}, so stopping
distance depends on the physics tick rate --- fine at a stable 60\,Hz, wrong under load.

\paragraph{Why it matters.} Two of the six parity bugs live here. \textbf{B2}: Godot

\subsection{Warning: Unity dead configuration}

Replicate \emph{behavior}, not asset values. Dead or misleading on the Unity side:
\code{MinSlopeAngle 50} (real steep threshold is the 46\textdegree\ slope limit),
\code{EdgeSlipSpeed 2} (slip is gravity-tilt), \code{StopTimeSec\,=\,0} (degenerate
SmoothDamp $=$ instant stop), \code{GroundDrag}, \code{MinImpulse},
\code{CharacterControllerRadius 0.4}, and the entire \code{ApplyAirDrag.cs} file (the live
one is \code{ApplyHorizontalAirDrag.cs}). The prefab serializes \code{StepOffset 0.3} but
code overwrites it with 0.35 every tick. \code{MaxSlopeAngle 80} \emph{is} consumed (wall
slide, slide animation, conditional rotation) --- just not for the steep-slope trigger.

\section{Reproduction checklist (test scene \code{-98,103})}

Derived from code, not yet executed live. Side-by-side, Unity vs Godot:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em, itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{B1} --- walk off a low ledge, jump within 0.15\,s: Unity jumps; Godot drops the input.
  \item \textbf{B2} --- approach thin elevated floors from below: Unity blocks; Godot passes through.
  \item \textbf{B3} --- walk into invisible blocker walls: Unity blocks; Godot walks through.
  \item \textbf{B4} --- release keys at full run under frame drops: Unity stops consistently; Godot varies with tick rate.
  \item \textbf{B5/B6} --- open glider below 1\,m altitude, then close and re-open quickly: Unity opens at 0.2\,m and re-arms in 0.2\,s; Godot refuses and waits 0.6\,s.
  \item \textbf{M1} --- walk into 0.35--0.5\,m ledges: Unity steps up; Godot blocks.
  \item \textbf{M2/M3} --- run up vs down a long ramp; stand on a $>$46\textdegree\ slope: Unity modulates speed and slides; Godot does neither.
  \item \textbf{M9/M10} --- tap vs hold jump: Unity arc differs; Godot arc is fixed and symmetric.
  \item \textbf{D1/D2} --- run-jump onto Unity-reachable geometry: different reachable set (1.5\,m @ 10\,m/s vs 1.8\,m @ 11\,m/s).
\end{itemize}

\section{Salvage decisions (recommendations)}

\paragraph{PR \#943 --- Rust locomotion rewrite + Jolt: drop the code, keep the body.}
Locomotion lives in \code{player.gd} and is iterated there; a Rust rewrite is the wrong
shape for incremental parity. The PR body, however, is an ADR-grade locomotion spec ---
reuse it as the implementation checklist for M1--M13.

\paragraph{PR \#1417 --- capsule + snap + slope + coyote + Jolt cherry-pick: re-scope
without Jolt and reopen.} The minimal parity slice. Split the engine change out, land the
GDScript-side fixes (coyote B1, snap/slope values) and the capsule fix via its own issue.

\section{Jolt evaluation}

Both dead PRs bundled the Jolt physics engine. Recommendation: \textbf{do not bundle Jolt
with parity fixes}. All six parity bugs and most missing systems are solver-independent
script logic. Jolt would improve contact robustness around thin geometry, but it does not
fix B2 (the one-way flag is our code, not the engine), and it imposes a cross-platform
revalidation cost. Bundling an engine swap with behavior changes makes regressions
unattributable --- the failure mode that killed both prior PRs. Re-evaluate Jolt only if
thin-geometry tunneling survives the B2 fix.

\section{Verification log}

Two independent adversarial validation passes were run against this document
(2026-08-25), each instructed to disprove every claim:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item An initial pass ran against a stale local unity-explorer checkout and produced
        false positives (``Unity has no air jump'', ``no jump cooldown'') --- both
        systems exist on \code{dev} with values matching Godot exactly. All Unity
        claims were re-validated against the updated checkout (\code{dev} @
        \code{6d8b5e966}).
  \item Validation also surfaced: the glide system on both sides with two real
        parameter mismatches (B5/B6); \code{MaxSlopeAngle} consumed (not dead) vs.\
        \code{EdgeSlipSpeed} dead; Unity physics running a manual variable timestep
        rather than fixed 50\,Hz; the layer-16 matrix contents
        (\code{SDKAvatarTriggerArea} in); and the dead \code{ApplyAirDrag.cs} file
        (live: \code{ApplyHorizontalAirDrag.cs}).
  \item Baseline claims from issue \#905: 13/13 Godot values verified exact.
        Corrections: hard-landing stun exists (scene-driven); moving platforms are
        partial (KINEMATIC switch without follow).
\end{itemize}
